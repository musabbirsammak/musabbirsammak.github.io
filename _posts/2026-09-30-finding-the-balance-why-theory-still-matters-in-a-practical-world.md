As a university teacher here in Bangladesh for the past few years, I often hear our education leaders talk about the need for more industry knowledge and practical skills. This is a great goal. We all want our students to be ready for the job market.

However, I have noticed that this push can sometimes lead to a misunderstanding. Sometimes, the focus shifts almost entirely to hands-on, vocational skills, causing both students and management to shy away from deep theoretical studies. For a while, I even started to agree with this shift.

My perspective changed during a year I spent at the University of Milan. There, the education was heavily focused on strong, rigorous theories. We definitely had practical work, but learning the core concepts deeply was absolutely required.

This experience led me to a personal realization, though I am always open to the possibility that I might be wrong. I believe that theory gives you the ability to see the future of your work. In my teaching journey, I have sometimes seen eager students start highly ambitious projects—for example, building complex machine learning tools—without really understanding the basic theories of how those systems actually work. Because of this missing foundation, these exciting projects often struggle or eventually fail.

If we spend the time to build a strong understanding of theory first, it can save us from investing our valuable time into ideas that might not work out in reality.

I am certainly not trying to discourage big, ambitious projects. We need that kind of energy and vision! I only want to humbly suggest that a good grasp of theory acts as a guiding light. It helps us channel our ambition into projects that are not just exciting, but also successful and built to last.
