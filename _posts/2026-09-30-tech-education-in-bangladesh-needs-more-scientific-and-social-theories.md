As a university teacher here in Bangladesh for the past few years, I often hear our leaders talk about the need for more practical skills. This is a great goal. We all want our students to be ready for the real world and the job market.

However, this push can sometimes lead to a misunderstanding. The focus often shifts almost entirely to hands-on and vocational skills. This makes both students and management shy away from deep theoretical studies. For a while, I actually agreed with this shift.

My view changed during a year I spent studying at the University of Milan. There, the education was heavily focused on strong theories. We definitely had practical work, but learning the core concepts deeply was strictly required.

This experience led me to a personal realization, though I am always open to the possibility that I might be wrong. I believe that theory gives you the ability to see the future of your work. In my teaching journey, I have seen eager students start highly ambitious projects without understanding the basic theories of how those systems actually work. Because of this missing foundation, these exciting projects often fail.

To give a specific example, I was once faced with a project pitch where an AI would automatically predict a student's future career based only on their answers to a few questions and their current grade transcripts. I sometimes joke that if an AI like that existed when I was an undergraduate, it would have probably told me to go beg on the streets!

It is a funny thought, but it points to a very serious issue. A project like that does not just lack computer science theory; it lacks an understanding of human life. This shows us that we do not just need strong theoretical foundations in science and technology. Theories in non-scientific areas like human behavior, society, and ethics are equally important, yet we have made them almost obsolete in our current system.

I am certainly not trying to discourage big, ambitious projects. We need that kind of energy. I only want to humbly suggest that a good grasp of theory acts as a guiding light. It helps us channel our ambition into projects that are not just exciting, but also meaningful, realistic, and built to last.
